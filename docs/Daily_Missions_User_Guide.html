USER GUIDE · ANDROID

Daily Missions

Your daily planner: check off missions, work toward goals, earn a medal for each day, and get reminders. Version 2.5.0, for Android 8 or newer.

Daily Missions keeps your routine for every day of the week, lets you check tasks off as you go, rewards each day with a medal, and helps you work toward bigger goals. Everything stays on your phone. There is no account, no internet connection, and no ads.

1. Install the app

You only need the file Daily_Missions_Android.apk. If you received a .zip file, unzip it first (step 2).

  1. Save the file to your phone. It might arrive in a text message, an email, Google Drive, or a chat app. Tap the attachment and choose Download if asked.
  2. If you got a .zip file: open the Files app (it may be called My Files on Samsung), go to Downloads, tap the zip file, then tap Extract. A folder named Daily_Missions appears with the app file and these guides inside.
  3. Tap Daily_Missions_Android.apk. You can find it in your notifications right after downloading, or in the Files app under Downloads.
  4. Android may say "For your security, your phone is not allowed to install unknown apps from this source." Tap Settings, turn on Allow from this source, then tap the back arrow.
  5. Tap Install.
  6. Google Play Protect may show "Unsafe app blocked" or "App scan recommended", because the app doesn't come from the Play Store. Tap More details, then Install anyway (on some phones it says Install without scanning).
  7. When it finishes, tap Open, or find Daily Missions on your home screen or in your app list. The icon is a calendar with a checkmark inside a gold ring.
Optional, for safetyAfter installing, you can turn the permission back off: Settings > Apps > Special app access > Install unknown apps, pick the app you installed from (for example Files or Chrome), and turn off Allow from this source.

2. Update the app

When you get a newer Daily_Missions_Android.apk, install it exactly the same way as above. Android shows "Do you want to install an update to this app?". Tap Update.

  • Your tasks, check marks, notes, settings, and passcode all stay. Do not uninstall first. Uninstalling deletes everything stored in the app.
  • Updates only install if they come from the same person, because every version is signed with the same key. If Android says "App not installed", see Troubleshooting.
  • After an update, open the app once so your reminders are planned again.

3. Moving from the old Morning Missions app

Only needed if your phone still has the very first Morning Missions app (version 1.0, with the old round logo). Daily Missions can't install over it, so move your data like this:

  1. Open the old Morning Missions app. Tap Save & tools (or Tools), then Export backup (.json). Save the file in Downloads.
  2. Uninstall the old app: press and hold its icon, tap Uninstall (or drag it to Uninstall), and confirm.
  3. Install Daily Missions (section 1).
  4. Open Daily Missions, tap Tools at the bottom, scroll to Backups, tap Restore backup, and pick the file from step 1. Tap Restore.

4. Quick start

  1. Open Daily Missions. It opens on today, with a starter morning routine already filled in. Saturday and Sunday start as rest days.
  2. As you finish each task, tap its checkbox on the left. The ring at the top fills up.
  3. Tap Tools at the bottom, find Notifications, switch it to On, and tap Allow when Android asks. Now you'll get reminders.
  4. Make the routine yours: tap + Add to add a task, tap a task's name to edit or delete it.
  5. At the end of the day, tap Finish day to see your medal.

6. Checking off tasks

  • Tap the checkbox on the left of a task to check it off. Tap it again to uncheck it.
  • The ring, the week strip, and the background pattern fill in as you go.
  • When every task for a day is checked, you'll see a short "complete" message.
  • You can check off tasks on earlier days too, for example if you forgot yesterday. Future days can't be finished yet, but you can look at them.
  • You can also check tasks off from the Week screen.

7. Adding a task

  1. Go to the day you want and tap + Add (top of the list) or Add task (bottom of the list).
  2. Type the task name. As you type, a list of tasks you already have drops down, with their usual time and days. Tap one to use it, and its time fills in automatically. Or keep typing to make a brand new task.
  3. Set Start and End. Tap a time to open the clock. When you change the start time, the end time moves with it so the length stays the same. The line under the times shows how long it lasts.
  4. Under Repeats on, tap the days it should happen (M T W T F S S). Shortcuts: Weekdays, Weekends, Every day, or This day only for a one-time task.
  5. Tap Save. A message at the bottom confirms it, with an Undo button if you change your mind.
Good to knowA new repeating task starts on the day you add it. Earlier days are not changed. If you pick days that don't include the day you're looking at, the task won't show on that day; the message tells you so. If a task with the same name is already on that day, a note warns you.

8. Changing or removing a task

  • Change the time: tap the task's time. The editor opens with the clock ready.
  • Change the name, days, or anything else: tap the task's name.
  • Remove it: tap the name, then tap Delete at the bottom left.

Repeating tasks: this day or every day?

When you change or delete a task that repeats, Daily Missions asks what you mean:

  • Change only [date]: just this one day. Other days keep the task as it was. The task shows CHANGED THIS DAY.
  • Change [date] and later: this day and every day after it. Days before it always keep what they had, so your history stays accurate.

Tick Remember my choice if you always want the same answer. You can change that later in Tools > Repeating tasks. Changing the Repeats on days always applies from that day on.

Made a mistake?After any change, delete, reorder, or sort, tap Undo in the message at the bottom of the screen. It stays for a few seconds.

9. Reordering tasks

  • Press on the grip (six dots) on the right of a task and drag it up or down. Or press and hold anywhere on the task for half a second until it lifts, then drag.
  • Moving a task never changes its time. To change a time, tap the time.
  • The new order applies to every day that has those same tasks.
  • Sort by time (the button above the list) puts everything back in time order.

11. Week screen

  • Tap Week at the bottom. You see all seven days as columns. On a phone, swipe sideways to see each day; today is shown first.
  • Check tasks off right in the columns.
  • Tap a day's dark heading to open that day.
  • Use ‹ and › to change weeks, and This week to come back.
  • Weekly record opens a summary of the week (section 19).
  • Week notes at the bottom are the same notes as on the Day screen.

12. Month screen

  • Tap Month at the bottom. Each square is a day: the more you did, the more orange it is. Rest means nothing was scheduled. A dashed square means there is no record, for example days before you started.
  • Tap any day to open it.
  • Use ‹ and › to go back through earlier months, and This month to come back.
  • Below the calendar: Done this month (your overall percentage), Perfect days (days at 100%), Best run this month (most perfect days in a row), and Current streak.
  • A small flag marks the last day of a goal's date group, and a trophy marks the day you achieved a goal.
  • The Goals card lists goals due this month, goals achieved this month, and what is coming up. Tap a goal to open it.
  • Weekly records lists each week of the month with a Record button.

13. Goals

Goals are bigger things you work toward over weeks or months, like running a 5K, reading 12 books, or saving for a bike. Tap Goals at the bottom to see them all. They also show on the Day screen and the Month screen.

Make a goal

  1. Tap Goals, then New goal. (Or tap New goal on the Day or Month screen.)
  2. Type a name, such as Run a 5K.
  3. Under Aim for a rough date, choose Month, Season, Quarter, or Year, then pick one from the list. The line under it shows the group name, the last day, and roughly how much time is left.
  4. Turn on one or more ways to measure progress (below), or none for a simple goal you mark achieved yourself.
  5. Tap Save.

Rough dates and groups

Goals with the same rough date are grouped together and named for it. Groups are sorted soonest first, and each shows the time left, like ~3 months left.

If a group's date passes before you finish, it says Time is up. Keep going, or open the goal and pick a new date.

Ways to measure progress

Use any mix of these. The goal's percentage is the average of the parts you turn on, and it reaches 100% when every part is complete.

  • Milestone steps: a checklist, like "Buy running shoes" and "Sign up for a race". Type a step and press Enter on the keyboard to add the next one. Tick steps in the editor, from Next step on the goal, or from All steps on the Goals screen.
  • Linked daily tasks: pick one or more of your repeating tasks and how many check-offs to aim for, like 40. Every time you check one of them off, from the day you made the goal on, it counts. A message shows the new count, and the task shows the goal's name.
  • A number you update: a start, a target, and a unit, like 0 to 3.1 miles, 200 to 180 lb (it works going down too), or 0 to 800 with $ as the unit, which shows as $800. Tap the number on the goal, then Update, to enter the new value. The −1 and +1 buttons nudge it.

When you reach a goal

The moment every part is complete, a trophy celebration plays with its own fanfare, and the goal moves to Achieved at the bottom of the Goals screen with the date and how early you were. If you later undo a part, such as unchecking a linked task, the goal opens again.

For a goal you finished another way, open it and check I have achieved this goal.

Change or delete a goal

  • Tap a goal's name to open it. Change anything and tap Save.
  • Delete removes the goal and its progress. Your tasks and check marks stay. Tap Undo if you change your mind.
TipLink a goal to a task you already do, like Workout, and set a check-off target. Then your normal daily check-offs move the goal along by themselves.

14. Notifications

Turn them on

  1. Tap Tools at the bottom and scroll to Notifications.
  2. Switch Notifications to On.
  3. Android asks "Allow Daily Missions to send you notifications?". Tap Allow.
  4. Tap Send a test notification. A notification should appear at the top of your screen within a second or two.

Choose which ones you want

Each kind has its own checkbox. Tap Turn all on or Turn all off to set them all at once.

NotificationWhat it doesYou can choose
Task remindersOne for each task you haven't checked off yet.When the task starts, or 5, 10, 15, or 30 minutes before
Morning summaryHow many objectives you have today, and which one is first.15, 30, or 60 minutes before your first task
Finish-day reminderAn evening nudge to tap Finish day and see your medal. Skipped once you've finished the day.Any time (9:00 PM to start)
Missed-task nudgeIf a task ends and isn't checked off, a reminder to check it if you did it.10, 30, or 60 minutes after it ends
  • Checking a task off cancels its reminder and its nudge.
  • Rest days with no tasks get no reminders.
  • Tap a notification to open Daily Missions.
  • Reminders keep working when the app is closed and after the phone restarts.
  • The app plans reminders two weeks ahead every time you open it. Open the app at least once every two weeks to keep them coming.

What shows on the lock screen

Under On the lock screen, choose:

  • Show task names: the notification shows the task name and time everywhere.
  • Hide task names on the lock screen: a locked phone only shows "Daily Missions: You have a reminder." The details appear after you unlock.
  • Never show task names: notifications only say things like "Mission at 6:15 AM", even when the phone is unlocked. This is the most private choice.

Fine-tune in Android settings

Go to Settings > Apps > Daily Missions > Notifications. Each kind (Task reminders, Morning summary, Finish-day reminder, Missed-task nudges) is listed separately, so you can change its sound, vibration, or turn it off there too.

Make reminders reliableSome phones, especially Samsung, Xiaomi, OnePlus, and Huawei, stop apps in the background to save battery, which can delay or block reminders. Set Daily Missions to unrestricted: Settings > Apps > Daily Missions > Battery and choose Unrestricted. On Samsung, also check Settings > Battery > Background usage limits and make sure Daily Missions is not in Sleeping apps or Deep sleeping apps.

15. Tools: name, look, and sound

Tap Tools at the bottom. The cards, from top to bottom:

  • You: Name on your records, shown on weekly records.
  • Look:
    • Theme: Auto follows your phone's light or dark mode, or pick Light or Dark. The round button at the top right of every screen switches it too.
    • Clock: 12-hour (6:15 AM) or 24-hour (06:15).
    • Sound effects: medal sounds On or Off.
    • Preview a medal: play any medal's animation and sound, including the Goal achieved trophy.
  • Repeating tasks: what happens when you change or remove a repeating task: Ask each time, Change only that day, or Change that day and later days.
  • Storage on this device, Notifications, App lock, Backups, Weekly records, and Start over: explained in the sections below.

16. App lock and privacy

Turn on the app lock

  1. Go to Tools > App lock and tap Set a passcode.
  2. Type a passcode of at least 6 characters, then type it again. A few words together is much harder to guess than a short number.
  3. Tap Turn on lock.

From then on, Daily Missions asks for the passcode when you open it, and everything it stores is encrypted with it. Backups and HTML copies are encrypted too.

  • Lock again after you leave the app: right away, or after 1, 5, 15, or 60 minutes away.
  • Lock now locks it immediately.
  • Change passcode needs the current passcode, then the new one twice.
  • Turn off needs the passcode and stores your planner without encryption again.
  • After 5 wrong tries, the app makes you wait before you can try again, and the wait grows with more wrong tries.
  • Hide from screenshots and the recent-apps preview: blocks screenshots and screen recording of the app, and blanks it in the list of recent apps.
Don't forget your passcodeThere is no way to recover it, not even by the person who made the app. If you forget it, tap Forgot passcode? on the lock screen to erase everything and start fresh. Keep a recent backup so you can restore your planner.
Notifications and the lockTo show reminders while the app is closed, the text of upcoming reminders is stored without encryption. With the lock on, choose Never show task names under Notifications if you want task names kept out of it.

17. Storage

Daily Missions saves everything on your phone automatically. Each day takes about 100 bytes, so a year of use is tiny. In Tools > Storage on this device:

  • The bar shows how much space it uses out of your limit.
  • Keep day details for: 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 2 years, or forever.
  • Storage limit: 100 KB up to 4 MB.
  • When days pass a limit: Keep only each day's score (for example "9/13", so the Month screen still shows how you did) or Delete them.
  • Ask me before archiving old days: if on, a message appears with Export backup first, Archive now, and Not now before anything is removed.
  • Check storage now checks right away.

18. Backups and switching phones

Your planner lives only on your phone. A backup is a single file that holds everything: tasks, check marks, notes, goals, and settings.

Make a backup

  1. Go to Tools > Backups and tap Export backup (.json).
  2. Android's file screen opens. Choose a place, such as Downloads or Google Drive, and tap Save.

With the app lock on, the backup is encrypted and its name ends in -encrypted. Export unencrypted backup appears too, but anyone with that file can read your planner.

Restore a backup

  1. Go to Tools > Backups and tap Restore backup.
  2. Pick the backup file. For an encrypted backup, enter the passcode it was made with.
  3. Read the summary and tap Restore. This replaces what's in the app now. Tap Undo if you change your mind.

Switch to a new phone

  1. On the old phone, export a backup and save it somewhere the new phone can reach, such as Google Drive, or email it to yourself.
  2. Install Daily Missions on the new phone (section 1).
  3. Restore the backup on the new phone.
Make a backup now and thenFor example once a month, and always before switching phones, resetting your phone, or uninstalling the app. Uninstalling deletes everything, and Daily Missions does not back up to the cloud by itself.

Save HTML copy saves the whole planner with your data inside as one web page file. You can open it in a browser on a computer to keep using it there.

19. Weekly records

  • A weekly record is a neat summary of one week: every task, a checkmark for each day you did it, your totals, goals achieved that week (and, for the current week, your active goals), and your week notes, laid out like a printed schedule.
  • Open one from Week > Weekly record, Month > Record next to any week, or Tools > Weekly records > View this week.
  • Download saves it as a file. Print / save PDF opens Android's print screen, where you can print or choose Save as PDF.

20. Start over

Tools > Start over > Erase all data deletes every task, check mark, note, goal, and setting, turns off the app lock and notifications, and brings back the starter routine. Export a backup first if you might want it back.

22. Good to know

  • Private by design. Daily Missions has no internet permission, so nothing can ever be sent off your phone. There are no accounts, ads, or tracking.
  • Goals keep their counts. When Storage archives old days, the check-offs they gave your goals are kept.
  • It saves as you go. There is no save button for your checks and tasks.
  • Themed icon. On Android 13 and newer, if you turn on Themed icons (in Wallpaper & style), the app icon matches your wallpaper colors.
  • Permissions it uses: showing notifications, setting reminders at exact times, and setting them again after a restart. That's all.
  • Storage while locked. With the app lock on, the app's own data is encrypted. If you also hide task names in notifications, nothing about your tasks is readable without your passcode.
